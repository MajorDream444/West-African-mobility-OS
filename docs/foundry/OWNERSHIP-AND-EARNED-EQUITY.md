## The governing sentence

> The platform creates opportunity and infrastructure. Ownership is earned through verified contribution, responsibility, risk, and results.

And its enforcement clause:

> **No ownership exists until an executed legal instrument exists.** Not on a ladder, not in a ledger, not in a conversation, not in this document.

---

## Why no percentages appear here

The most damaging thing this programme could do is promise a number early. A percentage promised before an entity exists is either dishonoured — poisoning the relationship permanently — or honoured on terms nobody understood, which is worse.

`AGENTS.md` rule 3 is absolute: never infer equity, partnership, exclusivity, territory, or authority from an introduction or group chat. This document extends it: **never infer equity from a ledger entry either.** The ledger measures contribution. The instrument creates ownership. They are different acts, done at different times, by different authorities.

## The contribution ledger

Eleven dimensions, each recorded with a source, a date, a validating authority, and an evidence class — the same discipline as the claims register, because a claim about a person's contribution is a claim.

| # | Dimension | Measured by | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Training completed | Verified credentials with external assessment | Attendance counted as capability |
| 2 | Customers acquired | Named, consented customer records with retention | Introductions counted as customers |
| 3 | Revenue generated | Booked and collected, attributed by a stated rule | Attributing team revenue to the loudest member |
| 4 | Assets managed | Asset value, uptime, and condition under their control | Managing an asset nobody could damage |
| 5 | Capital contributed | Verified contribution with source of funds | Promised capital treated as contributed |
| 6 | Risk accepted | Personal guarantee, loss exposure, unpaid build period | Claiming risk that someone else actually carries |
| 7 | Service quality | First-time-fix, rework, warranty quality, satisfaction | Volume without quality |
| 8 | Governance performance | Evidence discipline, gate compliance, honest reporting | Rewarding results achieved by breaking rules |
| 9 | Local team developed | Participants advanced a rung under their development | Claiming credit for the cohort's work |
| 10 | Intellectual property created | Curriculum, SOPs, tooling, standards, code | Restating existing doctrine as new IP |
| 11 | Ability to train successors | Successors independently certified | Indispensability mistaken for value |

**Dimension 11 is weighted deliberately high.** A founder who cannot be replaced is a single point of failure, not an asset. The corridor's core anti-extraction metric — local trainers certifying unaided — depends on this dimension being rewarded rather than punished.

### Ledger rules

1. **Sourced or absent.** An entry without a source, date, and validating authority is not recorded.
2. **The participant sees their own ledger** at all times, may dispute any entry through a defined process, and may export it permanently.
3. **Reconciled and countersigned** before any S6 instrument. An unreconciled ledger blocks the instrument.
4. **Contribution does not expire, but it does not compound by tenure either.** Time present is not a dimension.
5. **Negative entries exist** — falsified evidence, safety breach, governance breach — and are recorded with the same process rights as positive ones.
6. **The ledger is not a scoreboard published between participants.** Comparative visibility turns a development tool into a political instrument.

## The ten pathways

Each is a different answer to *what does this person actually own, and what risk do they carry for it?* The pathway is chosen by fit, not by seniority.

| # | Pathway | What is owned | Risk carried | Typical stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Founder-owned business licensed onto the platform** | Their own company entirely | All of it | S5 |
| 2 | **Country operating-company participation** | Shares in the country OpCo | Company-level | S6 |
| 3 | **Revenue-sharing service cell** | A share of the cell's revenue, not the entity | Performance only | S4–S5 |
| 4 | **Earned equity** | Vesting equity in a foundry venture | Vesting and forfeiture | S6 |
| 5 | **Management buy-in** | Purchased stake, financed or cash | Capital at risk | S5–S6 |
| 6 | **Franchised operating unit** | The unit, under corridor standards and brand | Unit-level operating and capital | S5 |
| 7 | **Cooperative ownership** | A member share in a jointly-owned entity | Shared, member-level | S4–S6 |
| 8 | **Driver-to-owner asset** | The vehicle, on a completed schedule | Payment and asset risk | S2–S5 |
| 9 | **Workshop-to-owner finance** | The workshop, tooling, and premises | Financed asset risk | S4–S6 |
| 10 | **Venture SPV participation** | A stake in a ring-fenced asset vehicle | Ring-fenced to that SPV | S5–S6 |

### Choosing a pathway

Three tests, in order:

1. **Can this person carry this risk?** Not "do they want to" — can they survive the downside? A driver-to-owner schedule that bankrupts a family in a bad quarter is predatory regardless of the headline.
2. **Does the ownership match the contribution shape?** Someone whose contribution is customers and quality wants revenue share or equity, not a financed asset. Someone whose contribution is operating an asset wants that asset.
3. **Is the risk ring-fenced correctly?** Heavy-asset pathways (8, 9, 10) require SPV structure so that one venture's failure does not contaminate unrelated participants' assets.

## Vesting, dilution, forfeiture, exit

These terms exist as **principles** here; the binding version lives in each executed instrument.

**Vesting.** Time-based vesting alone is rejected — it rewards presence. Vesting is milestone-based against the contribution ledger, with a minimum period so that ownership reflects sustained rather than burst contribution.

**Dilution.** Every participant is told, before signing, how future capital dilutes them and what anti-dilution (if any) applies. A participant who does not understand dilution has not given informed consent to an equity instrument.

**Forfeiture.** Limited to defined grounds: fraud, falsified evidence, serious safety breach, or material breach of the instrument. **Not** performance disagreement, not leaving, not raising a complaint, not declining a data use. Forfeiture requires a written finding, a right of response, a named decision-maker, and a recorded rationale.

**Exit and departure.** A departing participant's vested interest remains vested. Unvested interest is treated per the instrument. Departure does not forfeit credentials, ledger history, or portable records under any circumstances.

**Death and incapacity.** Vested interests pass per the instrument and applicable succession law. This is written into instruments at execution, not resolved at the worst possible moment.

## What is never permitted

1. Verbal or written promises of a percentage before an entity and instrument exist.
2. Ownership language on any public surface, portal, deck, or message — the language gate blocks it.
3. Treating a ledger entry, ladder rung, cohort membership, or gate pass as an ownership claim.
4. Allocating economics based on introduction, relationship provenance, family, or early presence. The provenance chain in `SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md` is **historical record, not an allocation**.
5. Instruments signed without independent explanation in the participant's fluent language.
6. Ownership offered as a substitute for wages that are actually owed.
7. Any solicitation of public investment.
8. Cross-collateralising a participant's personal assets against a venture without explicit, separately-advised consent.

## The Diallo precedent

The Diallo progression in `VISION.md` §6 is the first application of this document, and it is deliberately generalised into the founder lifecycle so that the terms are **published before they are needed** rather than negotiated under pressure with someone who has already delivered value.

That sequencing is the point. A ladder published before the climb is a system. A ladder negotiated after the climb is a dispute.
