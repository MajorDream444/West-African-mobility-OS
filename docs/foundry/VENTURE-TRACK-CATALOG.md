## How to read this catalogue

A track being listed means the foundry **recognises** the business shape and knows what shared services it needs. It does **not** mean a venture in that track is approved, funded, or wanted right now. Every venture still passes G1–G5.

Each entry names the **killing gate** — the stage at which ventures of this type most often fail. That is the most useful line in each entry: it tells a founder where to spend their evidence effort first.

**Capital shape** distinguishes *light* (tools, working capital, a founder's labour), *medium* (premises, equipment, a small team), and *heavy* (assets, fleet, inventory, facilities). Heavy tracks require ring-fenced SPV structures and are gated hardest, because they are where a failure takes other people's assets with it.

## Tier A — Service delivery (light capital, early cohorts)

### 1. Mobile mechanics
On-site service and repair at the customer's home, office, or roadside. Van or motorcycle-based, tool-kit driven.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, work orders, CRM, parts catalogue, credentials · **Capital:** light · **Killing gate:** G4 — parts availability at the point of work; a mobile unit without the right part is a wasted call-out.

### 2. Workshop modernization
Upgrading existing independent workshops: tooling, diagnostics, process, digital work orders, credentialing. Often a services business selling into workshops rather than a workshop itself.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, work orders, credentials, quality metrics, procurement pooling · **Capital:** light–medium · **Killing gate:** G2 — workshop owners' willingness to pay for process improvement is frequently asserted and rarely tested.

### 3. Automotive diagnostics
Specialist diagnostic centres and mobile diagnostic services for complex electronic, hybrid, and EV faults.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, work orders, vehicle records, credentials, quality metrics · **Capital:** medium · **Killing gate:** G3 — diagnostic tooling licensing and software access; equipment without current software is scrap.

### 4. Roadside assistance
Breakdown response, recovery, and emergency service, dispatch-driven, often subscription or fleet-contracted.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, work orders, CRM, fleet uptime · **Capital:** medium · **Killing gate:** G4 — response-time economics; the model lives or dies on density, not on price.

### 5. Fleet maintenance contracting
Contracted maintenance for institutional fleets: mining, logistics, government, NGO, banks, contractors.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, work orders, fleet uptime, parts, quality metrics · **Capital:** medium · **Killing gate:** G2 — procurement reality; institutional fleets buy on tender and payment terms, not on quality claims.

## Tier B — Trade, parts, and inspection

### 6. Spare-parts distribution
Parts sourcing, stocking, cross-referencing, and distribution to workshops and mobile operators.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, parts catalogue, supplier records, procurement pooling, landed cost · **Capital:** medium–heavy (inventory) · **Killing gate:** G3 — working capital and dead stock; the wrong SKU mix kills more parts businesses than demand does.

### 7. Vehicle inspection and certification
Independent pre-purchase, pre-import, and periodic condition inspection with a documented standard.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, vehicle records, credentials, evidence and claims · **Capital:** light · **Killing gate:** G2 — who pays: buyer, seller, financier, or insurer? Each implies a different business.

### 8. Certified used-vehicle commerce
Sourcing, inspecting, reconditioning, warranting, and selling used vehicles to a published standard.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, vehicle records, service history, landed cost, CRM · **Capital:** heavy (inventory) · **Killing gate:** G3 — Mauritanian used-vehicle imports are valued on a **minimum importation value, not invoice**; a model built on invoice price is wrong by construction.

### 9. Digital vehicle sales
Online discovery, comparison, and structured demand aggregation for new and certified used vehicles.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, CRM, vehicle records, landed cost, evidence gate · **Capital:** light · **Killing gate:** G2 — the language gate; this track generates public claims faster than any other and is the one most likely to publish an indicative price as a landed price.

### 10. Vehicle delivery and logistics
Vehicle movement, transporter services, port-to-customer delivery, inter-city relocation.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, work orders, vehicle records · **Capital:** medium–heavy · **Killing gate:** G3 — insurance and liability for vehicles in custody.

## Tier C — Asset and mobility operations

### 11. Taxi and driver-to-owner fleets
Managed mobility fleets with a structured pathway from driver to vehicle owner.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, fleet uptime, work orders, financing records, contribution ledger · **Capital:** heavy (SPV required) · **Killing gate:** G3 — the ownership schedule; a driver-to-owner plan that cannot survive a bad month is a rental contract with a story attached.

### 12. EV charging and battery services
Charging points, battery diagnostics, battery health certification, second-life handling, and safety.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, work orders, credentials, vehicle records · **Capital:** heavy · **Killing gate:** G2 — grid reality and demand timing; charging infrastructure ahead of the vehicle parc is a stranded asset.

### 13. Workshop equipment finance
Financing tooling, lifts, diagnostic equipment, and premises upgrades for independent workshops.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, financing records, credentials, quality metrics · **Capital:** heavy · **Killing gate:** G3 — constitution §3.10; no financing activity exists before entity, custody, contract, and risk allocation are approved. Regulated activity in both countries.

### 14. Insurance and claims coordination
Claims administration, assessment support, repair coordination, and evidence packaging for insurers.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, work orders, vehicle records, evidence and claims · **Capital:** light · **Killing gate:** G2 — insurer partnership; this business does not exist without a counterparty who wants it.

### 15. Fleet intelligence and telematics
Telematics installation, fleet performance reporting, uptime analytics, driver behaviour programmes.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, fleet uptime, vehicle records, quality metrics · **Capital:** medium · **Killing gate:** G3 — data rights; telematics collects continuous personal and location data on drivers and is the track most likely to breach `DATA-RIGHTS-POLICY.md` by default design.

## Tier D — Capability, inclusion, and node building

### 16. Technician education
Independent training providers, train-the-trainer, credential preparation, and specialist upskilling.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, training records, credentials, curriculum · **Capital:** light–medium · **Killing gate:** G4 — external assessment pass rates; a training business whose graduates fail independent assessment is worse than none.

### 17. Customer support and translation
Multilingual customer service, technical translation, documentation localisation (Arabic, French, English, Hassaniya, Wolof, Pulaar).
**Shared services:** Tier 0, CRM · **Capital:** light · **Killing gate:** G2 — who pays; usually a service to other corridor ventures before it is an independent business.

### 18. Women-led safe mobility
Women-operated and women-serving mobility: safe transport, women technicians, women-led workshops and service points.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, full Tier 1, inclusion monitoring · **Capital:** light–heavy by model · **Killing gate:** G2 — designing for the actual constraint (safety, schedule, childcare, social permission) rather than for the demographic label.

### 19. Local content and automotive media
Vehicle content, repair education, market information, and corridor storytelling in local languages.
**Shared services:** Tier 0, evidence and claims, language gate · **Capital:** light · **Killing gate:** G2 — the language gate again; media is the fastest route to an unsupported public claim.

### 20. Country-node development
Standing up the foundry model in a new country: legal, demand, service, governance, capital gates.
**Shared services:** all tiers, `country-envoy-onboarding` · **Capital:** medium–heavy · **Killing gate:** G1/G2 — no node inherits another node's authorisation, evidence, or regulatory position. Every gate is passed again locally.

## Sequencing guidance for Cohort 001

Start where capital is light, the killing gate is testable in weeks, and the shared services are already at Wave 1–2: **tracks 1, 2, 7, 16, 17**. These produce revenue and evidence early and fail cheaply.

Do **not** open tracks 8, 11, 12, or 13 in Cohort 001. They are heavy-capital, regulated, or SPV-dependent, and opening them before the platform, the entity, and the evidence base exist is how programmes of this shape lose other people's money.
