## Why this document exists

Two dossiers now answer the same ten questions. They were built differently: the 5 September dossier synthesises the project's own canonical sources; the 6 September dossier pulls from external primary and secondary sources. Where they agree, confidence rises. Where they disagree, one of them is wrong, and shipping both without resolution would put contradictory claims in front of Brother Ling, Diallo, and Chairman Tameo.

**One caution before the detail.** Convergence between the two dossiers is *not* independent verification wherever both ultimately trace to the same canonical sourcebook. The genuinely independent evidence is the externally sourced material. Several load-bearing numbers in the pitch — "99% Toyota", "7–8 million people", "300,000 taxi rides daily" — remain unverified from any primary source, and they are exactly the numbers a Chinese OEM or an investment committee will check.

## Part B — Contradictions resolved

### B-1. Macroeconomic figures — v1.0 supersedes

| | 5 Sep dossier | 6 Sep dossier |
|---|---|---|
| GDP growth 2024 | 5.2% | 6.3% |
| Forward view | 4.9% average 2025–2027 | 4.2% (2025), 4.4% (2026f), 4.5% (2027f) |

**Resolution:** Both cite the World Bank. The 6 September figures come from the current *Macro Poverty Outlook*; the 5 September figures come from an earlier publication. Same authority, later date → **the 6 September figures govern.** Use 4.4% for 2026, population 5.3 million, and note the ouguiya moved only 0.13% against the USD in 2025.

### B-2. Used vehicles — **I was wrong, and the red team is right**

My v1.0 stated that the removal of Mauritania's age limit "strengthens the certified-used lane, because there is no regulatory wall keeping used stock out." That inference was backwards.

The red team's position is correct: used vehicles bring hidden collision damage, variable specifications, unpredictable parts, and — critically in Mauritania — **customs valuation on an administrative minimum value rather than invoice**, which means margin is unknown until the vehicle clears the port. You cannot run a scalable business on an unknown landed cost.

**Corrected reading:** the age-limit removal is a *competitive threat* signal, not an *opportunity* signal. It means cheap aged stock enters freely, which (a) compresses the premium available on new vehicles, and (b) makes a formal certified-used business compete against informal importers carrying almost no cost base. Both effects push the same way — **toward the service and parts layer and away from the vehicle lane.**

**Governing position:** start with new vehicles only, to establish technical credibility, predictable parts, and a clean warranty path. Revisit certified-used only after HAMAL's inspection and provenance layer is operational and the administrative minimum-value schedule is in hand. This aligns v1.0 with the sourcebook's original sequencing, which I should not have drifted from.

### B-3. Marine Foundation's position in the entity chain — **v1.0's structure should govern**

The 5 September entity diagram places Marine Foundation **above** the Corridor Venture Company, as the top box in the chain. My v1.0 memo places it **parallel to and outside** the commercial chain entirely.

The 5 September document's own red team supplies the argument against its own diagram: *"using a foundation to front a commercial car business looks incredibly sketchy to an investment committee."* An organisation chart showing the Foundation as parent of the commercial holding company is precisely that picture. It also undermines the four bright-line tests, because a parent entity is structurally connected to its subsidiaries' liabilities and governance in ways a convening partner is not.

**Governing position:** Marine Foundation sits outside the ownership chain, connected by named, dated, scope-limited authorizations for specific educational and convening activities. Redraw the diagram before it appears in any briefing to Chairman Tameo or any investor.

### B-4. Deposits: refundable or non-refundable — the 5 Sep sources contradict themselves

The 5 September dossier's Q8 allocates customer-cancellation risk to the OpCo "through non-refundable, contractually backed deposits held in escrow." The red-team transcript from the same body of work calls for "non-trivial **refundable** deposits held securely in escrow."

**Resolution:** non-refundable consumer deposits, taken before a legal entity, refund policy, and import pathway are approved, are a consumer-protection exposure and — in some framings — a deposit-taking exposure. **Refundable, escrowed, and only after counsel** governs. This is consistent with `DEC-009`. Note also that escrow and non-refundability sit awkwardly together: escrow exists to protect the payer.

### B-5. Driver default — `DEC-012` governs

The 5 September Q8 allocates driver default to the SPV "via automated telematics tracking, localized guarantor networks, and structural repossession rules."

That is close to the Moove design, which produced defaults, repossessions, and driver protests. **Guarantor networks in particular push default risk onto the driver's family and community** — a recognisable extraction pattern that would fail the project's own anti-extraction test at questions 6 and 9.

**Governing position** (`DEC-012`, unchanged): payments sized against evidenced driver net earnings; maintenance carried by the SPV; a documented cure period before repossession; accrued equity retained rather than reset. Telematics is fine as an asset-tracking tool. Guarantor networks should be struck.

### B-6. Workforce counts — both are right, at different evidence grades

My v1.0 said no count exists. The 5 September dossier supplies detailed regional figures. Both hold: those figures are **planning estimates from an internal report, not a census**, and the 5 September document says so itself ("no reliable regional census or registry count").

Absorbed as `ASSUMPTION`-grade planning ranges across 16 West African countries:

| Metric | Range | Central |
|---|---|---|
| Active four-wheel vehicles | — | ~22.1 million |
| Service locations (mostly informal) | 44,000–74,000 | — |
| Working technicians | 100,000–260,000 | ~155,000 |
| Formally/OEM-qualified | — | ~34,000 (~22%) |
| **Modern Chinese-vehicle-ready** | **3,000–16,000** | — |
| Realistically upskillable | 45,000–115,000 | — |

**The decisive number is the fifth row.** Against 100,000–260,000 working technicians, only 3,000–16,000 are ready for modern Chinese platforms. That gap *is* the corridor's thesis, quantified. It also confirms that the workshop and technician registry recommended in v1.0 is not duplicative work — the 5 September plan's own "150 workshops mapped" line is the same instrument.

## Part C — New material absorbed into v1.0

### C-1. Senegal's hybrid taxi programme is already live — and it changes Q9

`VERIFIED` Senegal's **Land Transport Development Fund (FDTT)** and the **Bank for Trade and Industry (BCI SN)** are jointly deploying **1,000 hybrid taxis**, with the first batch of **100 vehicles arriving February 2026**. Drivers receive a managed acquisition plan with management, maintenance, and technical-monitoring requirements, explicitly designed to build creditworthiness and pull operators into the formal financial sector. **Vehicle suppliers are not named in the reporting.**

This is the single most actionable finding in this reconciliation, for four reasons:

1. **It is the corridor's own Senegal taxi thesis, already funded and executing** — same vehicle count for the first tranche (100), same hybrid-first powertrain call, same driver-financing-toward-ownership structure, same formalisation goal.
2. **It began roughly seven months ago.** The corridor is not early to this idea in Senegal; it is late.
3. **A named government fund and a named bank are the counterparties** — which is exactly the kind of written-commitment partner Q9 asks for, and far more concrete than the institution list v1.0 said had to be built from scratch.
4. **No supplier is publicly named.** Whether that tranche is sourced, and by whom, is a checkable question with commercial consequence.

**Consequence for the corridor:** Senegal is either a **supply-and-service opportunity into an existing funded programme**, or it is **competition with a head start and state backing**. It is no longer a greenfield hub. The right next action is not to design a Senegal taxi pilot — it is to find out who is supplying and servicing the FDTT/BCI SN fleet, and whether the corridor can serve it.

### C-2. The World Bank project is real, and its scope is closer to the corridor than expected

`VERIFIED` The **Dakar Sustainable Urban Mobility Project**, approved **17 June 2025**, is **USD 100 million in IDA concessional financing**, covering the Dakar metropolitan area with preparatory studies for secondary cities, benefiting **3.8 million people**. Components include BRT–TER integration, restructured bus networks, low-emission buses, traffic management, climate-resilience infrastructure — and, directly relevant, **"professionalization of informal transport operators through training and fleet renewal,"** intended to move thousands of workers from informal into structured employment.

The 5 September dossier flagged this project and correctly noted it is often misreported as an IMF fund. Confirmed: it is World Bank IDA. **Its training-and-fleet-renewal component is the corridor's workforce thesis, already financed.** That is an alignment opportunity, not a funding source — the corridor holds no relationship to it and must not imply one.

### C-3. Mauritania's Investment Code excludes the corridor's core activities from incentives

`REPORTED` (APIM, via the 5 September dossier): the Investment Code **excludes ordinary purchasing of goods for resale, and activities governed by banking rules including equipment leasing**, from incentive coverage.

This partially closes v1.0's `OPEN` O-11, and it is materially bad news that should be surfaced rather than buried: **vehicle trading and fleet leasing — two of the corridor's three main revenue lanes — likely get no investment-code tax relief.** Training facilities, workshops, and technology may qualify; trading and leasing likely do not.

Two consequences: the entity design should place qualifying activities (training, workshops, technology) where incentives can attach, and the landed-cost model must not assume any incentive relief on the vehicle lane. **Still `OPEN`:** the primary Investment Code text (UNCTAD returned 403; obtain via APIM or counsel).

### C-4. Regional Jetour distribution map

`REPORTED` Ghana — **Swiss Group**, authorised distributor. Nigeria — **Jetour Nigeria** network. South Africa — authorised networks operational. Senegal and Cape Verde — **Salvador Caetano** (`VERIFIED` independently).

`OPEN` **Mauritania has no confirmed authorised Jetour distributor.** That territory gap is the corridor's actual opening with the OEM, and it is a far more defensible ask than any regional exclusivity claim. Brother Ling's supplier mandate should be narrowed to three written questions: who controls Mauritanian rights; which configurations are factory-approved for the market (LHD, French/Arabic interface); and whether factory warranty survives import through this channel.

### C-5. Frameworks worth adopting

**The Six Hard Gates** (government mandate → anchor demand → China supply docs → technical readiness → locked economics → pilot authorisation) are a better-specified gating system than the loose gates in v1.0. Adopt them, with one amendment: **Gate 1 as written risks implying government endorsement before it exists.** Restate as *"written confirmation of the sponsoring office, or documented confirmation that no mandate exists."* The honest negative must be an acceptable Gate 1 outcome, or the gate creates pressure to overstate.

**The Four Levels** (Personal → Commercial → Industrial → Sovereign) is a genuinely useful organising frame, and its diagnosis of the origin — a personal vehicle request mistaken for an institutional opportunity — is the clearest short statement of what this project is. Worth keeping as the narrative spine of any briefing.

**Model D train-the-trainer** (5 lead Mauritanian technicians certified in Senegal or China → they train the remaining 15, with Mandarin materials translated to Arabic/French/English) is concrete and testable. Adopt, and attach the knowledge-retention test from the architecture memo: if capability degrades when the Chinese master technician leaves, transfer did not occur.
